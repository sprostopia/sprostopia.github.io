Lawnmower

Columns and rows.
How splendid!
Neat rectangles:
Perfect scars.
Filtered smooth 
by the good nature 
of a long distance.

How I long to be the passer-by
admiring from afar…

To count the number of cells
you excelled at rendering
onto the grass table 
that embellishes
your impeccably groomed front lawn!

Yet surely you look closer after
you’ve accomplished such a feat?

Stop to admire
the heads of your freshly decapitated
daises
poking out
from
beneath
the rubble?

Only you get close enough
to truly appreciate 
your handiwork,

aside from the passing jogger perhaps,
sneezing and spluttering
like a Lawnmower,
feverish from the spores:
nature’s pathetic defence.

You get down on your knees
and run your fingers 
through the scattered remains 
of the lawn’s hideous locks,
Wince and smile
at its prickly touch,
at the roughness of what’s left 
— shaven to perfection.

It’s yours.

The pests that mocked you,
invading and seducing your property,
have been neutralised.
Some were mown down
but the rest
all flew or
crawled,
slithered or
staggered,
limped,
shuffled or
rolled
to the other lawn across the road:

Growths of green erupt in humps, scattering bursts of vile weeds across its surface:
some flaunt pretty petals, colourful and naïve,
others stand slumped, wilted and brown.
Yet all contribute to the shattered kaleidoscope of disarray churning with the wind,
Flowers swaying and retching,
butterflies flailing and tumbling:
a complete excess of colour,
clashing, uncertain—
the lawn left weak to the unrelenting, all-consuming chaos of the wild.
The undergrowth beckons to your refugees:
A life of festering between the knots,
clambering over each other in piles,
promised freedom
but unable to escape the mould.

Dreams,
not nightmares,
consume you.
The same every night:

You strike a match—
it escorts you through the shadows
getting darker,
blacker than oil,
deeper than the silence
your boots penetrate
as you inch closer
to the neighbour’s lawn.

You look down upon a slug,
no—
a cockroach
hauling itself across the asphalt,
trailing
moonlight
in its wake…

You spot another trail,
then another—
crossing the first;
they migrate in cobwebs
spreading like a fungus,
tangled, glimmering,
surging forwards
like a net of disco-ball fish
fighting fate.

You inhale violently;
fumes unfurl inwards
filling your lungs with diesel,
rising from the tendrils
of your Lawnmower’s secretion
now soaking hell’s creatures,
concluding their exodus in slime.

You’ve let go of the match:
it spins as it falls.

Your light fades.

A terrific surge of gold
devours the night,
unrelenting,
racing across the lines,
sprawling,
spitting embers,
chasing prey
with bullish fury.

The fleeing sponges vanish in puffs.
Wisps ascend, their souls left grounded.
It’s followed the clues—
the world-eater;
with a triumphant roar
the insects are forgotten.

The lawn is purged,
reduced to charcoal
destined to litter
your neighbour’s foul earth.
